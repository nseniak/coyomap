**Where a step arriving through a door is anchored — one rule, for every agent that writes or reads
one.** A step that comes IN through an interface (`In → Cn`: a web address, an API route, a tool, a
command reaching our code) is anchored at the `source` line of the way in THIS step comes through,
whatever that line looks like: a route line, a route decorator, a tool handler's definition, a
command's declaration. That line is where the arrival happens, so it is the one step anchor that may
sit on a definition header, and the checks accept it there. Take the way in this step comes through,
not the use case's first one, when a story moves between two. A step going OUT (`Cn → In`) is
anchored where the component delivers: the return, the render, the write. A skeptic confirms a real
arrival with that way-in line as its `evidence`, even when the work happens further down: the work is
the next step's claim, with its own anchor.
